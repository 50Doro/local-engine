The two hours that cost you the job

You already know the job. The number is the hard part.

Not because you can't do the work. Because the price has to come out of a walkthrough, a rate you half-remember, and a feeling about what the last guy charged.

"It's about eighteen hundred square feet. Call it thirty-five hundred."

Round numbers are where the money goes. Rounding rarely lands in your favor. Two hours of real takeoff can be the difference between a 41% margin and a 26% one — and you will never know which one you just quoted.

"Can you do any better on the price?"

You say yes because you don't have the cost in front of you. On a $2,478 job at 40% margin, knocking 10% off the price does not cost you 10%. It costs you a quarter of the profit. Most contractors discover this after the check clears.

"I've got two more guys coming out. Can you email me something?"

So you type a paragraph into an email at 9pm, with no scope, no terms and no third option. The other guy sends a page with his logo on it. Price was never the only difference.

Quote Engine exists to close that gap: the takeoff, the price and the proposal are the same piece of work, done once. Measure the room in front of you, and the price and the page are already there.

How it works

Three steps, in the order you already work.

Nothing here is a new process to learn. It is the takeoff sheet you already write on a clipboard, with the arithmetic done for you.

01

Measure the surfaces

One row per surface. The surface type decides how the row is measured, and that is the only rule:

  • Area — walls, ceilings, siding, decking. Qty × width × height, minus a deduction for the windows and the fireplace.
  • Linear feet — trim, fascia, cabinet boxes, fence. The tool converts to square feet.
  • Each — doors, windows, drawers, cabinet doors, garage doors. Count them and move on.

132 line items across 18 trades — painting, pressure washing, roofing, flooring, fencing & decks, landscaping, drywall, handyman, cleaning, lawn care, mobile detailing, plumbing, HVAC, electrical, auto repair, locksmith, appliance repair, and a blank Custom trade. Five condition factors (standard through worst case) and a coats-or-passes override per row. Three to five preset scopes per trade fill in the usual job so you are editing instead of starting from blank.

02

Read the money rail

It builds from the bottom: labor hours × your burdened wage, then prep and setup, then materials with waste, then sundries, then your overhead. The total is your cost — the number you have to beat, not the number you quote.

Your price comes from one formula, the same one every time: price = cost ÷ (1 − margin). A 40% margin, not a 40% markup. They are not the same number and the difference is exactly what pays for the rework day.

03

Print the page

Seven proposal themes, one Letter page, your logo text at the top and your pitch at the bottom. Three honest scope tiers built from the same takeoff, so the difference between them is defensible line by line. Print to PDF and it's in their inbox before you're home.

The part that changes your pricing

Margin is not markup. This is the part that pays for the tool.

On $1,000 of cost, a 40% markup prices at $1,400 and leaves you $400. A 40% margin prices at $1,667 and leaves you $667. Same word in the van, different number on the invoice — and the difference is a full day of somebody's labor.

Quote Engine only speaks margin, because margin is the number that survives a slow week.

Why the numbers are yours

Every rate is editable, and calibrating takes one job.

Each trade ships its own production and material rates, rounded from commonly published estimating ranges — interior walls 150–250 sq ft per hour per coat, ceilings 125–200, brushed trim 50–100 linear feet per hour, airless spray 600–1,000, doors and windows about 45 minutes each; tear-off 1.5–2.5 squares per hour; LVP 100–150 sq ft per hour; mowing 3,000–5,000 sq ft per hour. They are labeled as starting points because that is what they are, and every one of them is editable.

Then you replace them:

Measure your hours the way you measure the job.

Start the clock when the crew begins setup. Stop when the truck is loaded. Write it down the same day. Then: sq ft/hr = (area × coats) ÷ hours. Do that for three jobs with the same surface, take the middle number, and type it into the Rates tab.

After three jobs the tool is quoting your company — your crew, your prep standard, your climate — instead of an average of the internet. The manual walks through the whole table, including the two numbers to check after every job: quoted hours versus actual hours, and billed rate per crew-hour.

Why it's built this way

Three decisions shaped this, and they were all made against the usual advice.

It is one file, not a web app. A subscription needs a server, a login and a connection. A contractor on site needs none of those. So the entire tool — fonts, styles, logic — is inside a single HTML file that will still open in ten years.

Questions

The ones that get asked

Do I need to install anything?

No. It is an HTML file. Double-click it and it opens in Edge, Chrome or Safari. On Windows, if it opens in Notepad instead, right-click → Open with → Edge, and check "Always use this app".

Does it work without internet?

Yes — that is the point. The fonts are embedded in the file. It makes zero network requests, so it works in a truck, a basement or a house with no service. Once downloaded, you never need a connection again.

Where do my jobs and client details go?

Into your browser's local storage on your own computer. Nothing is uploaded and nobody sees it, including whoever sold you the file. Export writes a .json backup you can move between machines or keep forever.

What happens if I clear my browser data?

You lose the saved job — so export after every job that sells. The file itself is unaffected. Import brings a job back on any computer.

Can I put my logo on the proposal?

Upload your logo once on the Rates tab and it prints at the top of all seven themes. Your business name, phone and one-line pitch print too, in the theme's own typography. Use a clean PNG or SVG so it scales.

Will a big job fit on one page?

Yes. The proposal measures itself and scales down to stay on a single Letter page, and tells you when it has done so. It will not silently spill onto page two and make you look careless.

Is it US-only?

It is built in feet, square feet and US dollars — there is no unit or currency switch, and I would rather tell you that than let you buy it and find out. The math works anywhere; the labels and the money format do not. If you are pricing in metres and euros, this is not the right tool yet.

Do I get updates?

Version 1.0 is a finished single file that will keep working. If a corrected build is released, license holders get it at no cost through the email on their receipt. Updates are not promised on a schedule.

Can I share it with my crew or a friend?

Your own crew, on your own computers: yes, that is one business. A friend's business: no — that is a separate license. It is $47; please don't pass it around.
